# 1. Introduction

## What This Application Does
[Current Codebase Implementation]

This software is an interactive educational and research application for exploring automated electrocardiogram (ECG) signal processing. It provides:

1. **Faithful Pan-Tompkins Signal Processing**: A complete implementation of the dual-signal adaptive thresholding QRS detection pipeline originally developed by Jiapu Pan and Willis J. Tompkins in 1985.
2. **Step-by-Step Visualization**: Interactive inspection of all five algorithmic stages: Raw ECG, Bandpass Filtering, Derivative Filtering, Pointwise Squaring, and Moving-Window Integration.
3. **Downstream QRS Morphology Delineation**: An application-specific heuristic layer operating on the raw ECG to estimate morphological landmarks ($Q$ point, $R$ peak, $S$ point, $QRS_{\text{onset}}$, and $QRS_{\text{offset}}$ / J-point).
4. **Interactive Playback Engine**: Real-time simulated playback (0.25×–2.0× speed) with beat-by-beat tracking and single-step sample navigation.
5. **Synchronized 3D Cardiac Conduction**: An interactive Three.js 3D anatomical heart displaying cardiac conduction pathways (SA node, AV node, His bundle, bundle branches, Purkinje network) synchronized with cardiac phases.
6. **MIT-BIH Reference Evaluation Benchmark**: A dedicated validation module using deterministic minimum-cost bipartite matching to evaluate detection performance against physician-annotated MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database records according to ANSI/AAMI EC57 standards.

## What Is ECG Analysis?
[Cardiovascular Electrophysiology Background]

An **Electrocardiogram (ECG)** records the minute electrical potentials generated by heart muscle cells (myocytes) during each cardiac cycle, measured via electrodes on the patient's skin.

A standard cardiac cycle consists of:
* **P wave**: Atrial depolarization (atrial contraction).
* **PR segment**: Conduction delay through the atrioventricular (AV) node.
* **QRS complex**: Rapid ventricular depolarization (ventricular contraction).
* **ST segment & T wave**: Ventricular repolarization (ventricular electrical recovery).

Automated ECG analysis transforms continuous digitized voltage signals into clinically meaningful diagnostic events, intervals, and rhythm classifications.

## Why QRS Detection Matters
[Cardiovascular Electrophysiology Background]

The **QRS complex** is the most distinctive and prominent feature of the surface ECG. Detecting the QRS complex reliably is the foundational first step in almost all automated cardiac signal processing algorithms because:

* **Heart Rate Computation**: Instantaneous heart rate ($HR = 60 / RR$) and heart rate variability (HRV) metrics (such as SDNN) are derived directly from the temporal distance between consecutive QRS occurrences.
* **Arrhythmia Detection**: Identifying irregular intervals (e.g., atrial fibrillation), premature ventricular contractions (PVCs), and missed beats requires precise beat timing.
* **Morphological Anchor**: Delineation of lower-amplitude features (such as P-waves, PR intervals, and QT intervals) relies on the QRS complex as the primary temporal fiducial anchor.

## Educational and Research Purpose Notice
[Application-Specific Design Choice]

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Not a Medical Device or Clinical Monitor**
> This application is designed strictly for educational, instructional, and algorithmic research purposes.
> * It provides **interactive playback and offline processing** of recorded digitized signals; it is **not** a real-time clinical bedside monitoring system.
> * Morphological landmarks ($Q, R, S$, onset, offset) are heuristic estimates intended for visual exploration and educational demonstration.
> * This software must **never** be used as a primary diagnostic tool, clinical decision support system, or in life-critical patient monitoring scenarios.
